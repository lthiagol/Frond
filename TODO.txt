    diff; the audit rounds then found the same class twice more (the third

    written out five times and the copies had already diverged enough to
    hide a real bug for a release.
  - A background writer in a shared slot clobbers what the user needs to
    see: the watch warning was written into the sticky error slot, so it
    never cleared and replaced a refused write within a second. A
    persistent state needs its own slot and its own precedence.
  - A test that asserts "it moved" cannot catch "it stops short" — assert
    the end positions, not the motion. The scrollbar bug survived a
    release because of it.
  - ratatui's ScrollbarState wants the scroll RANGE as its content length,
    not the line count. The two differ by the viewport, which is why the
    thumb was short and stopped short of the track end.
  - The release notes are the orchestrator's, and they must cover every
    user-visible change — the 0.6.4 review caught a whole audit round
    (the documentation fixes) that had shipped unnoted. Check the notes
    against the findings list, not against the diff.
  - The Makefile assumed `cargo` was on the PATH. It now falls back to
    rustup's default location.
  - A rule that grows has to grow its enumeration too: giving the selection
    rule one home pulled the disambiguation popup into it, and the spec and
    the changelog still named six panels. Both reviewers caught it from
    their own lenses; the enumeration is part of the rule.
  - Two gates at once fail each other: the smoke suites append to fixed
    paths (`/tmp/watchtest.md`), so a concurrent `make check` makes the
    other's line-count check fail. The orchestrator's gate and the
    builder's gate must not overlap - one gate at a time.
  - A review brief can describe the code's polarity backwards (the unit 30
    brief said the bare `re:` case replaced the active search where the
    code clears it). The builders follow the code, and the reviewers said
    so; write the brief from the code, not from memory.
